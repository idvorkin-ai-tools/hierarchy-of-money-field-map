The Hierarchy of Money, mapped

Gregory Gundersen's village parable is the best plain-language account of how money actually works: stones become notes, notes become reserves, a village's money becomes the world's. This map starts with the illustrated story — his wording, our animals — and turns that story into various explainers, one idea at a time.

The spine

Five layers. Each exists because the one below went rigid. If you read nothing else, read this list — every explainer on this map unpacks one of these.

  • 1 · CommodityA villager could not settle a debt by making more promises. Stones are money because they are hard to collect. Credit and interest are stacked on top the first time someone needs boots today and stones next year. Intermediation
  • 2 · Bank moneyA bank could not settle a debt by creating more of its own deposits. The raccoon writes a line and the bull has a note. Notes are money between villagers. They are not money between banks. Creation
  • 3 · ReservesThe tortoise issues money that banks will accept from each other. Harvest drains stones, overnight loans fail, the system gridlocks, and debt moves up. Elasticity and a single blunt rate. Centralization
  • 4 · Reserve currencyOne village could not settle a debt by creating more of its own currency. The fox defaults. The crow's money becomes the habit, then the peg. Exchange
  • 5 · FiatAnd yet nothing happened. Convertibility ends. Taxes, wages and contracts stay in black money. The floor was a promise, and the building was never resting on it. Fiat
